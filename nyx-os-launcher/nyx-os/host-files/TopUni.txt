Things NOT to do when applying for Top Universities!
1. Don’t Exaggerate or fabricate your activities!
a. Admissions officers go through thousands of applications each cycle, and they've
developed a sixth sense for spotting exaggerated or fabricated activities.
Faking/Exaggerating your achievements can severely damage your credibility and
harm your chances of admission because the Admissions officer will not believe
you anymore.
i. For example, let's say you participated in an internship during your
sophomore year of high school. In reality, you might have dedicated 5-6
hours a week to this internship. However, for the sake of enhancing your
college application, you claim that you committed 20 hours a week on top
of your full-time school schedule and several other extracurricular
activities. An admissions officer can easily spot this discrepancy. They
understand the demands of a full-time high school schedule, especially
when coupled with after-school activities. Claiming a 20-hour-a-week
internship in addition to all of that raises red flags because it's simply not
feasible for most students to manage. Therefore, it's very important to
present your extracurricular involvement accurately and avoid
exaggeration. Instead, focus on the quality of your experiences and what
you learned from them, rather than trying to impress with inflated time
commitments.

2. Don’t treat the Recommendation Letters lightly!
a. Choose your Teachers wisely for your Recommendation Letters!
i. The recommendation letter shows the Admissions officer how you are in a
classroom enviornemnt. They look for traits that would be a good addition
to their University. Stanford Ex-Admissions officers say that Admissions
officers work for the Professors of the University. Hence, they are
choosing students that Professors will want!
ii.
Therefore, it's crucial to choose your recommenders wisely. Opt for
teachers who know you well and can provide a detailed, personalized
account of your abilities and growth over time, rather than those who may
hold a prestigious title but have little insight into who you truly are. If you
are very close with the teacher I even recommend you to take a look at
your letter before Submission as this is something I did!
1. When selecting a recommender, think about those who have seen
you grow, whether in academic settings, extracurricular activities,
or even challenges you’ve overcome. Their insights can paint a

3. Don’t write a generic Personal Statement Essay!
a. Your personal statement is the most important piece of writing!!!!
i. This is your opportunity to show that you are more than numbers.
This essay puts a face to all the “numbers” you have in your
application. This is where you convey your unique story, personality,
and aspirations to the admissions committee. Writing a generic essay
that could apply to anyone is a missed opportunity and can make
your application blend in with thousands of others. Admissions
officers are “bored speed readers” who read countless essays every
cycle, so a generic, cookie-cutter approach won't make a lasting
impression.
ii. For example, imagine an essay that starts with a cliché like, "I've
always wanted to help people," followed by a broad, impersonal
recounting of various volunteer experiences. While the sentiment is
positive, this approach is overused and will make admissions officer
roll their eyes before they move on to the next application.
Admissions officers are looking for essays that delve deeper into
your personal experiences and reflect your unique voice. They want
to understand what drives you, what challenges you've overcome,
and how these experiences have shaped your perspective and goals.
b. The lesson is: START YOUR ESSAYS NOW!!!! It took me 28 drafts to get
a “perfect” essay. The more you write and the more comments you get the
better your essays will get. So, do not leave it till the last second!
